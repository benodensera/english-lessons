Vocabulary ⏱ 8 min

Study these 10 key words. Press 🔊 US next to each word to hear its pronunciation directly on this page.

Word Part of speech Definition Example sentence
fare
noun The money you pay to travel on a bus, train, taxi, etc. The bus fare went up by 20% this year.
route
noun The path or road used to travel from one place to another. We took the scenic route along the coast.
commute
verb / noun To travel regularly between home and work or school. She commutes two hours every day by train.
transfer
verb / noun To change from one vehicle or line to another during a journey. You need to transfer at Central Station.
platform
noun The raised area at a train or subway station where passengers board. The train to the airport leaves from platform 4.
congestion
noun Heavy traffic that makes movement slow or difficult. There is serious congestion on the highway every morning.
departure
noun The act of leaving; the time or place when a vehicle leaves. Check the departure board for your gate number.
pedestrian
noun / adj. A person who is walking, especially in a city; relating to walking. The new pedestrian bridge connects the two parks.
schedule
noun / verb A timetable showing when vehicles arrive and depart. According to the schedule, the next bus is at 9:15.
carpool
verb / noun To share a car journey with others, usually to save money or reduce traffic. Four colleagues decided to carpool to reduce costs.

Practice tip: Cover the "Definition" column and try to explain each word from memory. Then check.

Reading ⏱ 10 min

Read the text carefully. Then answer the comprehension questions below.

Getting Around the City

Maria lives in a large city and uses several forms of transportation every week. On weekdays, she takes the subway to get to her office. The subway is fast and avoids the terrible morning congestion on the main roads. She buys a monthly pass, which makes the fare much cheaper than paying for each trip separately.

On Tuesdays and Thursdays, Maria cycles to work. She enjoys the fresh air and the exercise, and her company has a bicycle parking area and showers for employees. She says the route along the river is beautiful, though she has to be careful near pedestrian crossings where walkers and cyclists share the same path.

On weekends, she sometimes carpools with her friends to visit places outside the city. They share the cost of fuel and parking. Maria believes that choosing greener transportation options – like cycling, public transport, and carpooling – is important for reducing air pollution and traffic.

Her one complaint? The departure times of the last subway train are too early. "If the schedule ended at midnight instead of 11 p.m., I wouldn't need to take a taxi home after evening events," she says.

Comprehension questions:
  1. Why does Maria prefer the subway over driving on weekdays?
  2. What two things does Maria's company provide for cyclists?
  3. What does Maria do to travel outside the city on weekends?
  4. What is Maria's main complaint about public transport?

Grammar: Comparative & Superlative Adjectives ⏱ 10 min

When we talk about transportation, we often compare different options. That's where comparative and superlative adjectives come in.

Adjective type Rule Example
Short (1 syllable) Add -er / -est fast → faster → the fastest
Ending in -y Change y → ier / iest busy → busier → the busiest
Long (2+ syllables) Use more / the most convenient → more convenient → the most convenient
Irregular Special forms good → better → the best  |  bad → worse → the worst
📝 Useful patterns:
• The subway is faster than the bus.
• Cycling is cheaper than taking a taxi.
• The train is the most comfortable option in this city.
• Rush hour traffic is worse than usual on Fridays.
Exercises:
  1. Complete with the comparative form:
    a) The metro is ________ (fast) than the bus.
    b) Taxis are ________ (expensive) than shared rides.
    c) Cycling is ________ (good) for the environment than driving.
  2. Complete with the superlative form:
    a) This is ________ (busy) train station in the country.
    b) The ferry is ________ (slow) way to cross the river.
    c) Electric cars are ________ (efficient) vehicles on the market.
  3. Write TWO original sentences comparing two types of transportation you know.

Role-play / Speaking ⏱ 10 min

Practice this role-play. If you're alone, play both roles — read Person A's line, then respond as Person B. Record yourself if possible.

🧍 Person A — Visitor

You are a tourist in a new city. You need help getting to the central museum.

  • Ask how to get there by public transport.
  • Ask about the fare and how long it takes.
  • Ask if there are transfers needed.
  • Thank the person and confirm the platform or stop.

🧍 Person B — Local

You know the city well. Help the tourist.

  • Recommend taking the subway (Line 2, 3 stops).
  • Mention the fare (~$1.50) and travel time (~12 min).
  • Tell them to transfer at City Hall stop.
  • Warn them about congestion during rush hour.
Useful phrases for this conversation:
  1. "Excuse me, could you tell me how to get to…?"
  2. "The easiest way is to take the subway / bus number…"
  3. "You'll need to transfer at…"
  4. "The fare is about… / It takes around… minutes."
  5. "Watch out for rush hour — the trains get very crowded."
  6. "You're welcome! Have a great visit."
