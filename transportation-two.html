Warm-up ⏱ 5 min

Think about the last time you used public transport. Answer these questions out loud or in writing.

  1. Was your last trip on public transport on time or delayed? What happened?
  2. What is the biggest problem with public transport in your city — cost, delays, crowding, or something else?
  3. Have you ever complained to a bus driver, train conductor, or transport worker? What did you say?
  4. Which do you think is worse: waiting 20 minutes for a delayed bus, or paying a very high fare? Why?

Goal: Warm up your English brain and connect today's topic to your own experience.

Vocabulary ⏱ 8 min

These 10 words are essential for talking about public transport problems. Press 🔊 US to hear each word.

Word Part of speech Definition Example sentence
delay
noun / verb A period of time when something is late or postponed. There was a 30-minute delay on the metro this morning.
overcrowded
adjective Containing too many people; uncomfortably full. The bus was so overcrowded that I couldn't get on.
refund
noun / verb Money returned to a customer because of a problem or cancellation. I asked for a refund after the train was cancelled.
complaint
noun A statement that something is unsatisfactory or unacceptable. She filed a formal complaint about the service.
cancellation
noun The act of deciding that a planned event or service will not happen. The cancellation of the last train left us stranded.
rush hour
noun The busiest time of day when many people travel to or from work. Avoid travelling during rush hour if you can.
unreliable
adjective Not able to be trusted or depended on; often late or broken. The bus service is so unreliable that I prefer cycling.
peak fare
noun The higher ticket price charged during busy travel periods. The peak fare on Friday evenings is twice the normal price.
season ticket
noun A pass that allows unlimited travel for a fixed period, usually cheaper overall. A monthly season ticket saves me about $50 a month.
disrupt
verb To interrupt or prevent something from continuing normally. Heavy snow disrupted train services across the region.

Practice tip: Cover the "Definition" column. Try to define each word from memory using the example sentence as a clue.

Grammar: Comparatives & Superlatives in Context ⏱ 10 min

In Part 1 you learned the basic rules. Now let's go deeper — using comparatives and superlatives to complain, compare services, and give opinions about transport.

Structure Use Example
not as … as Show something is less than another The bus is not as fast as the metro.
much / far + comparative Emphasize a big difference The train is much more expensive than the tram.
getting + comparative Show something is changing over time The delays are getting worse every month.
the + superlative + I've ever… Express a personal record or extreme That was the longest delay I've ever experienced.
less + adj + than Reverse comparison (less formal alternative) The tram is less crowded than the bus.
📝 Complaint patterns — very useful for speaking:
• "The service is getting worse and worse."
• "This is far more expensive than it used to be."
• "The buses are not as frequent as they were last year."
• "That was the most unhelpful response I've ever received."
Exercises:
  1. Rewrite using the structure in brackets:
    a) The metro is faster than the bus. → The bus is ________ (not as … as)
    b) The delays are very bad and they increase every week. → The delays are ________ (getting + comparative)
    c) I never waited so long before. → That was ________ (the + superlative + I've ever)
  2. Complete with the correct form:
    a) The new tram line is ________ (reliable) than the old bus route.
    b) Fares are ________ and ________ expensive. (getting / comparative)
    c) This is ________ (bad) cancellation I've ever seen.
  3. Write a short complaint (3–4 sentences) about a real or imaginary transport problem in your city. Use at least TWO structures from the grammar box.

Pronunciation: Stress in Compound Nouns & Phrases ⏱ 5 min

Many transport words are compound nouns (two words together). In English, the stress almost always falls on the first word. Listen and practise.

Word / Phrase Stress pattern Notes
rush hour
RUSH hour Stress on first word — always
season ticket
SEA-son TIC-ket Both words stressed, first more so
peak fare
PEAK fare Stress strongly on first word
cancellation
can-cel-LA-tion 4 syllables — stress on 3rd
overcrowded
o-ver-CROWD-ed Stress on 3rd syllable
unreliable
un-re-LI-a-ble 5 syllables — stress on 3rd
Practice activity:
  1. Say each word or phrase 3 times, clapping on the stressed syllable.
  2. Record yourself saying: "During rush hour, the overcrowded trains cause unreliable service and frequent cancellations." Play it back — does your stress sound natural?
  3. Minimal pair challenge: say delay (noun) and delayed (adjective) — can you feel the extra syllable?

Role-play / Speaking ⏱ 10 min

This role-play is a complaint situation. If studying alone, play both roles — pause between each turn. Record yourself if possible.

🧍 Person A — Passenger

You've been waiting 40 minutes for a bus. You missed an important meeting. You want a refund or explanation.

  • Explain the situation (delay, overcrowding).
  • Say this is the worst service you've ever experienced.
  • Compare it to last month — it's getting worse.
  • Ask for a refund or compensation.
  • Ask why there was no announcement or apology.

🧍 Person B — Customer Service Agent

You work for the transport authority. You must deal with the complaint professionally.

  • Apologise for the delay and inconvenience.
  • Explain the cause (technical fault, road works).
  • Offer a voucher (not a cash refund — company policy).
  • Suggest the season ticket as a cheaper alternative.
  • Tell them off-peak hours are less crowded and cheaper.
Useful phrases for complaining politely in English:
  1. "I'd like to make a complaint about…"
  2. "This is far worse than it used to be."
  3. "I've been waiting for over 40 minutes — that's completely unacceptable."
  4. "I'm afraid that's not good enough. I'd like a full refund."
  5. "Could you explain why there was no announcement?"
  6. "I understand, but this is the third time this week."
